# Compiled reasoner: results

The experiment in [docs/compiled-reasoner.md](../../docs/compiled-reasoner.md), run as written, plus one baseline the doc lacks: **Ada with learned weights and pairs** (`ada_learn`: `glyphh_spec.Model` on the world's spec, `learn()` then `calibrate()` on the world's training cases; the kernel's own learning since 3.5). Generated by `research/compile/run.py`; the section headed *Reading* at the end is written by hand.

**Learners.** `knn`: `Space` (k=15, min_sim 0.10), record then `query()`. `ada`: `Model` on the world's spec, nothing learned (exact weighted sim). `ada_learn`: the same after `learn()` (role and value weights, up to 5 learned pairs of roles) and `calibrate()`. `logreg`: logistic regression on the glyph, C by 3-fold CV inside the training cases. `mlp`: one hidden layer of 256, early stopping on a slice of the training cases. `icl`: the transformer as the doc specifies (glyph projected to d_model 128 as a soft token, outcome embedding added, plus a rank embedding; 4 layers, 4 heads; k=16 precedents from `Space.query` receipts + the query), trained once across worlds, never on an evaluation world. `icl_sim`: the same plus each precedent's kernel similarity to the query (a number every receipt already carries) as an input feature. A kernel learner whose query finds nothing above min_sim answers with its store's majority outcome, counted right or wrong.

**Worlds.** Every name fresh per world (layer, segments, roles, values, outcomes). 3-6 roles, vocabularies of 3-6, a numeric 0..9 role for threshold×category (the spec's thermometer bins). Two-hop: a selector role says which of two nested segments' role `x` decides. Noisy: one of the other five with 10% of stored outcomes flipped; graded against the rule. Each world holds out two value-combinations of the two roles its rule reads (single: its role and another): `cases` = held-out cases of seen combinations, `combos` = cases of held-out combinations. Every evaluation world is unseen by the transformer (held-out worlds), so the transformer's every number here is on held-out worlds; its accuracy on the training worlds' own reserved cases is given below for the gap.

Numbers are % correct, mean over worlds with a 95% interval (1.96 × standard error over worlds). Seeds fixed; nothing was chosen on evaluation data: every variant run is reported.

## Reading

**Verdict: no-go.** All four variants (as specified and with the similarity feature, at 4096 and at 1024) fail
criterion 2, the one that decides it: on XOR and two-hop the transformer lands within about a point of kNN (90.4-91.6
vs 90.9-91.6 on XOR, 90.8-92.4 vs 91.6-91.7 on two-hop) instead of 5 points above it, and it trails `ada_learn` by 8
points on XOR (≈91 vs 99.5). Three of the four also miss criterion 1, on held-out combinations: single-role
78.6-83.2 vs kNN 85.7-87.4 (and `icl_sim` at 4096 on noisy, 62.3 vs 66.8). All pass criterion 3 but `icl_sim` at 4096
(the flip: 103-107 precedents vs kNN's 105-110; that one needs 115), and all pass criterion 4 (2.0 ms at 4096, 1.2 ms
at 1024 on CPU, against the 10 ms bar). By the doc's closing rule ("if it doesn't beat logistic regression anywhere,
the kernel's algebra is already doing the work") it doesn't: logistic regression is ahead of it on held-out cases
(95.5 vs 93.6) and on held-out combinations (70.3 vs 65.1), and the kernel's own learning is ahead of both.

**What the transformer learned is kNN.** Its answers agree with `Space` kNN's on 90% of cases at 4096 (93% at 1024),
more than with any other learner (86-88% with `ada_learn` or logreg), and its accuracy, online curve and flip curve
follow kNN's within noise at every point. The doc's limit 1 said it would be "better reasoning over the neighbours,
not wider recall"; it isn't even better reasoning: it's a vote over its 16 neighbours. The seen-world vs. unseen-world
gap (95.2 vs 90.1 at 4096) shows where the extra capacity went: memorising the training worlds' fresh symbols, which
carry nothing to a new world. Validation accuracy reached ~90% within the first 300 steps and stayed flat through
30,000, so more steps wouldn't change the result. 1024 is no worse than 4096 (94.5 vs 93.6 overall), which fits a
model that only uses neighbour similarity, not symbol detail.

**The learned-pairs comparison is the headline.** `ada_learn` (`Model.learn()`: information-gain role weights, value
weights, up to 5 pairs of roles, then `calibrate()`) is the best learner on held-out cases in every family at both
dims: 98.8 overall vs kNN 93.7, logreg 95.5, MLP 97.2, transformer 93.6. On XOR it reaches 99.6, because `learn()`
finds the rule's pair of roles in 100% of XOR and AND worlds and 97% of threshold worlds, and the pair turns XOR into
an exact match on the pair's value. It learns online too: about 6 points over kNN and the transformer at 16 and at
128 precedents, with a counting pass and no gradient. It's also the best-calibrated kernel learner (ECE 3.9 vs kNN
5.6). Where it's weak is what pairs can't do: **held-out combinations**. A pair value never seen has no value weight
and matches nothing, so on AND worlds' unseen combinations `ada_learn` falls to 56.5 (kNN 75.9, logreg 93.9).
Two-hop is barely touched by pairs (the rule pair is found in 10% of worlds; a selector-dependent read is a
three-role relation), so there it lands near kNN (94.6 vs 91.7 on cases, 56.3 vs 56.9 on combinations).

**Held-out combinations defeat everyone on XOR and two-hop.** On XOR every learner is below chance (12.7-27.9%): an
unseen combination's neighbours share one of its two values and, under XOR, mostly carry the opposite outcome, so
similarity points the wrong way. Two-hop held-out combinations sit at 41-58%. No learner here abstracts "this value
is in the same half as that one" or "x means the same in either segment", and that includes the transformer. That
abstraction is what the proposal hoped for, and the result says a kNN-context transformer of this size doesn't
acquire it from synthetic training.

**Flip.** No learner sees time, so every one needs about as many new precedents as there are old ones near a case
(~100-115 after 100 old ones). The transformer isn't overriding context with priors (it tracks kNN), but it doesn't
do better either. `ada_learn` is the most stubborn right after the flip (8% follow the new rule at +0, since its
learned weights amplify the old rule) and the quickest once relearned (93% at +300 vs kNN 79%).

**Calibration.** The transformer's ECE is low (1.1-3.3) only because about 90-94% of its answers sit in the 90-100
bin. Below that bin it's overconfident (85% stated, 67-79% right), the opposite of kNN, which is underconfident (85%
stated, 98% right).

**Budget.** World pools: 3,000 training worlds (981,949 cases) and 120 validation worlds per dim, encoded with the
kernel in ~30 s (1024) and ~45 s (4096) on 10 processes. Training: 30,000 steps × 256 episodes per variant (a 300-step
timing run gave ~25 ms a step), 10-15 min each on `mps`, 47 min for the four. Baselines: ~10 min for every per-world
fit on 9 processes; transformer evaluation ~2 min. One training seed per variant.

**Caveats.** One training seed per variant. The noisy family's rule is any of the other five, so its numbers mix
them. Held-out-combination test sets are small in some worlds (XOR worlds with few cases), hence the wide intervals.
The logreg and MLP online and flip curves retrain at every point: they're references, not online learners.
`ada_learn`'s online and flip curves run `learn()` + `calibrate()` at every point, which is what `refresh_every` does
in a live model.

**What next.** Don't build the compiled reasoner. The evidence points at the kernel's learning instead:

1. **Pairs that generalise.** `ada_learn` already wins on interactions; it loses on unseen pair values. Back off when
   a query's pair value has no learned weight (weigh the pair 0 for that query, so its roles' own weights decide),
   and try learned *value classes* (group a role's values by the outcome distribution they carry, so "v1 and v3 are
   the same half") as the abstraction that held-out combinations need. Both are cheap to test on these worlds: the
   harness takes any learner with add/refit/predict.
2. **Three-role structure.** Two-hop needs a pair conditioned on a third role (selector × segment value). A bounded
   search over role triples in `learn()`, scored by information gain like pairs, is the next test, with the same
   held-out-combination split to check that it generalises.
3. **Recency.** Every learner needs ~100 new precedents to undo 100 old ones because none weighs time. A recency term
   in the vote (the kernel already stamps every precedent) is a direct test on the flip worlds.
